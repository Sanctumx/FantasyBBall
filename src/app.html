Courtside Draft Kit

How the board works

Logging picks

Type each pick and press Enter or Taken, or press Taken on his row in the player list. The pick always goes to the pick on the clock, so when it's your turn it's yours automatically and otherwise it goes to the team picking. The draft order puts every pick on the right team, so the Teams panel fills itself in. Undo pick takes back the last one; tap a pick in the recent list twice to take back an earlier one, and the next pick you log goes into that spot.

Zscore

A player's season value against an average drafted player. Each category is scaled by how far apart teams finish, measured by simulating drafts in which every team follows ADP. Categories where teams end up bunched together reward small edges more.

−1 and −2 Zscore

Zscore with the player's worst category, or worst two, left out, the way he looks on a team that punts them. The dropped categories show in the cell. Edge and the rank number follow whichever Zscore is shown.

Season, per game or mix

Season counts each player's projected games, so a player who misses time is worth less. Per game gives everyone the same games (the drafted-pool average), so value comes only from production when he plays. Mix sits halfway: each player counts as playing the average of his projected games and the pool average, which gives exactly the average of his season and per-game stat lines, so missed games count half. This choice drives Zscore, Gain C and Edge.

Gain C (Gain Categories)

How much a player raises your chance of winning each category against the other teams in this draft, compared with leaving the pick to a waiver-level player. Your forecast is your picks plus a typical player at each pick you have left; every other team's is its picks plus a typical player at each of its remaining picks. Unpicked slots count as twice as uncertain as a single typical player. It changes with every pick, so a third big man adds less once you have two. Numbers are hundredths of a category win, averaged over your opponents: 72 means he adds about three-quarters of a category win.

Gain M

Gain Matchup. Instead of adding up category chances, it asks how much a player raises your chance of winning the whole matchup against each other team (more categories than he wins, ties counted half), averaged over every opponent. Numbers are percentage points: 10 means a 10-point better chance to win the matchup. It rewards strength in categories that are close and gives little for piling onto categories you already win or have no chance in. It follows the Scoring setting: weekly swings in H2H, season totals in roto.

Head-to-head

In H2H each week is a fresh contest, so the board adds each category's week-to-week swing. Swing comes from game-to-game variance in each stat plus the number of games a player gets in a week. Here is each category's weekly swing relative to how far apart teams sit in a week; higher means a lead there is less reliable, so it counts for less:

H2H most categories values the chance of winning the week. A category counts for more the more often it decides your matchup.

The # order and Model pick

The # column ranks available players by whatever "# order by" is set to: Gain C, Gain M or the Zscore shown. The Model pick badge works underneath and always uses Gain M, whatever # order by is set to: it marks the best pick that is most likely to get to you. It weighs each player's Gain M by his chance of still being there at your next pick, then checks your following pick, so if a slightly better player is likely to last, it picks the one who won't. When you're on the clock every player is 100% to be there, so with # order by Gain M it usually matches #1. Players who probably won't reach your pick have a grey name. With Hide taken off, taken players appear where their value would place them, without a #, so the numbers still rank only players left.

There at / Lasts to

The chance a player is still available at your next pick and at the one after, given he is on the board now. They come from his ADP and how much draft position varies, which grows the later a player goes. Safe until is the last pick he has an 80% chance to reach; Gone by is the first pick where that falls to 20%. Rank range is ADP plus or minus one standard deviation.

Edge

Fantrax ADP minus the pick his value says he deserves, in picks. Positive means he is going later than he should. Edge vs picks the yardstick: Zscore uses his rank on the Zscore shown; Gain C and Gain M use picks made so far plus his rank on that metric among players still available, so they change with every pick and are blank for taken players. An ADP later than the last pick of your draft counts as the pick after it (marked *), so undrafted players don't look like bargains.

ADP

Fantrax ADP from the projections file. The two players without one are treated as late picks with a wide range.

Fantrax sync

Every league you connect to is saved, with your team, in a list under the league ID box: pick one and press Connect to switch, or Remove to forget it. Paste your league ID (or the whole league URL) and press Connect. The board reads your draft from Fantrax every 10 seconds, sets the team count, rounds and draft order from your league, and puts each pick on the team that made it, traded picks included. Choose your team so Gain C and availability are worked out for you. You can still log picks by hand; anything Fantrax reports takes priority. A drafted player missing from the projections is added as a waiver-level player.

Playoff games

How many NBA games each player's team plays during your fantasy playoffs, from the 2026-27 schedule. Set when your playoffs start and how many weeks each round lasts in League Settings; the small numbers are games per round. Green means at least one game above the league average, red at least one below. Week 1 is opening week (Oct 20–25) and later weeks run Monday to Sunday. With the All-Star break merged, Feb 15–28 counts as one week and later week numbers drop by one. Playoff boost, above the player list, turns this into value: each player's multiplier is 1 + strength × (his playoff games ÷ the league average − 1), kept between ×0.50 and ×1.50, with strength 25% on Light, 50% on Medium, 75% on Strong and 100% on Very strong. At 100%, 12 games against an average of 10.6 is ×1.13. It scales Gain C, Gain M and Zscore (on top of any scarcity adjustment), so the # order, Model pick and Edge follow. Off leaves values unchanged. Hover a Playoff games cell to see a player's edge and multiplier.

Stats

The Stats dropdown picks what the nine category columns show. Gain is how much a player raises your chance of winning each category right now. Zscore is each category's z-score times its category weight, so the nine add up to the Zscore column. Raw is his projected per-game line (FG% and FT% as decimals). In Raw and Zscore the chips are colored by his z-score in that category, so turnovers are red when he commits many. Sorting by a category column follows the choice.

Category weights

Each category has a weight for Zscore, like Basketball Monster's punt settings but adjustable: ×0 punts it, ×0.5 counts it half, ×1 is normal and ×2 counts it double. The weights apply only to Zscore and the −1 and −2 versions (and so to anything you set to follow Zscore, such as Edge vs Zscore or # order by Zscore). Gain C, Gain M and scarcity always count every category, since they measure your real chances of winning. Gain C already adjusts to your roster: if you've drafted poor free-throw shooters, the FT% gain from new players shrinks on its own. Reset sets everything back to ×1.

Scarcity

Category scarcity measures, for each category, how much value is lost between the best players left now and the ones likely left at your following pick. A category drying up fast scores above 1×. Position scarcity compares the lineup slots teams still need at each position (about 1.5 per team at PG, SG, SF and PF and 2 at C) with good players left who can play there. The list shows the two separately: Scarcity Cat is his strongest categories weighted by their scarcity, and Scarcity Position is the scarcest position he can fill. Both update after every pick, and the line above the list shows every category's and position's current index as a pill: red from 1.15× (drying up), amber in between, green at 0.85× or less (plentiful). Two dropdowns adjust values, one for each: the Scarcity Cat dropdown uses the Scarcity Cat column and Scarcity Pos uses the Scarcity Position column. Each multiplies Gain C, Gain M and Zscore by 1 + strength × his score, where strength is 25% on Light, 50% on Medium, 75% on Strong and 100% on Very strong, kept between ×0.50 and ×1.50. At 100%, a +20 score means ×1.20. With both on, the two multipliers are applied one after the other, not averaged. The # order, Model pick and Edge follow. Off leaves values unchanged; hover a scarcity cell to see its multiplier.

IL spots

With IL spots you can move an injured player off your roster and add a replacement, so the games he misses aren't zeros. The board counts a share of each player's missed games (35% per IL spot, up to 90%) at the production of the best players left after the draft. That helps injury-prone stars: with 2 IL spots Embiid moves from about 100th to 65th by Zscore. In backtests scored with daily lineups (10 active), 2 IL spots and 4 adds a week, Mix plus IL fill won 2.3 points more matchups than Mix alone; Season totals did far worse (17 points), because streaming and IL fill-ins make games played matter less than per-game production.

Correlated categories

Matchup win chances account for the fact that a team playing more games that week tends to win the counting stats together (and lose TO). Each team's weekly totals share a games factor (rostered players' weekly games vary by about 1.4, measured on 2024-26 logs), and the board averages the matchup over the games gap. This made the predicted win chances better calibrated; it did not change picks much.

Back-to-back rest

Some players sit the second night of back-to-backs. With B2B rest On (in the player list controls), a player's playoff games drop by his rest rate times his team's back-to-backs in your playoff weeks (from the 2026-27 schedule), and Playoff boost uses the lower number. Rest rates come from the last two seasons of game logs: how often he played the second night compared with his other games. Players who sit at least a quarter of them show a small B2B mark. Click a player's Playoff games cell to mark him as always sitting back-to-backs, always playing them, or back to the data.

Board users

Set how many of your opponents draft with this board. By default the board expects every opponent to pick near Fantrax ADP. Opponents on the board take players by value instead, so good-value players go earlier than ADP says. The board blends the two when working out who will still be there, the Model pick and the punt forecasts. In backtests where every opponent used the board, telling it so turned Auto from 2.8 points worse than Punt Off into 9.8 points better.

Punt strategies

Plain Gain treats your future picks as typical players at their ADP, so it charges a punt in full and never credits the payoff: later picks chosen to stack the other categories. For each strategy (no punt, and every single, double and triple punt: 130 in 9-cat) the board instead forecasts your open picks as players near their ADP who fit it best, and works out your average matchup win chance if you follow it from here. The Strategy line shows the best six. Punt Off keeps plain Gain. Auto blends the Gain of no punt, the single punts and the 8 most synergistic double punts (pairs where players weak in one category tend to be weak in the other, measured on the current projections) by how far ahead each is. In backtests, letting Auto pick among all doubles and triples chased punts that only looked good by chance, so the rest are for locking; their pills have dashed borders. With Auto, Gain stays neutral early and leans into a punt as your roster commits to it. Picking a strategy in the Punt dropdown, or clicking its pill, locks Gain to it; a punted category then counts zero in Gain C. Category weights still only affect Zscore.

Colors

Category cells are chips: green when a player helps you in that category, red when he hurts, deeper the bigger the effect compared with the best of the 50 top available players; under half a point shows as a plain 0. Zscore uses the same chips, scaled to the largest Zscore among the top 50 available players. Gain C, Gain M and Edge are green when positive and red when negative. Playoff games is green at least one game above the league average and red at least one below. There at and Lasts to are green from 75%, amber from 40% and red below that.

Saved drafts

Name the draft and press Save to keep a copy: picks, settings, Fantrax league and team. Load brings a saved draft back onto the board, Delete removes one and New draft clears the picks while keeping your settings; each asks for a second tap when it would lose something. Saved drafts live in this browser only, so they won't appear on another device or browser.

Limits

Lineup positions and games caps aren't modeled. Your draft is saved in this browser only.
