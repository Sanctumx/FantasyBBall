How the Team tab works

Everything here is a preview of your roster. Nothing is saved or sent to Fantrax; Reset roster puts back the saved team.

The roster table

Projected remaining games (GL)

The games the player is projected to play from now to the end of the NBA season (not just the league's last regular week): his team's remaining games times his projected games over 82. That already covers injuries and rest, so Plays % is not applied to it.

Plays %

A measure of your team's makeup, not of the player's health. Each night only as many players as the league's active limit can start, so with more players than slots the best per-game players take the slots and the rest sit. Plays % is the share of his team's remaining games he gets a slot for: your best players are near 100%, your worst get the least. Reserve is only a parking place: its players show 0% and count for nothing, so park a player there to see the team without him. Fill Active ranks every non-IL player by Win M and puts the best ones (up to the roster limit) in Active, the rest in Reserve. Per game compares everyone at peak non-injury performance, so IL players get 0% and GL is the team's full games left; on Rest of season Active and IL players are there for their projected share of games (games over 82) and Plays % is the share of those games he gets a lineup slot for.

Source

Which lines judge the players. Projections (Average, BBM or Hashtag; the default is the average) are what the Win numbers were calibrated on. Season to date and the recent-games windows (last week, 2 weeks, month) use the 2026-27 box scores: stats, Zscore, Plays %, Win C and Win M all follow, but games left still come from the projections, and the Win numbers from recent games are uncalibrated (small samples swing them). A player with no game in the window keeps his projection line and is marked proj. Switching source keeps your swaps and the Changes list; the Rankings tab has its own Source.

Stats and the Plays % box

Per game shows each player's projected line. Rest of season multiplies it by his projected games left. With "Adjust player stats by Plays %" checked, every player's stats and Z are also multiplied by his Plays %. Unchecked, the player rows are untouched and only the team values use Plays %.

Moving players

Click any column header to sort inside each group (click again to reverse, a third time to clear). MPG is his projected minutes per game. Each row has a slot select (Active, Reserve, IL); you can also drag a row onto another group's header. Moves are a preview, and Plays %, the team average, Win C, Win M and the matrix all follow. Active cannot go over the roster limit: a move into a full Active is refused. Active is the league's active spots plus the bench; at most 10 of them play each night, so the extra ones sit some nights, as Plays % shows.

Team average

The last row of the table. It always uses Plays %: a player adds his line in proportion to how often he plays, per lineup slot. Its Z is the same Plays %-weighted average of the players' Z; its Win C and Win M are your average over all opponents (the Average column of the matrix). IL players add nothing.

Points leagues

When Settings has Scoring on points, the Zscore view, the category weights and Win C are replaced by fantasy points from your point values: each player's line times the values (Pts/g, or Points over his games left in the Rest of season view; the Plays % box applies as before). Win M is the chance to win the week when each team's week is one points total: the spread comes from the engine's own stat noise plus measured correlations between stats, and a crowded night goes to the better points player. The matrix shows each team's points in an average week and your Win M against it; the card shows your points per week. The table's last row is still your average per lineup slot. Win M is not yet calibrated on real points weeks.

Win C, Win M and the matrix

The matrix is your previewed team against every other team in the league, one column each. A category cell is your chance to win that category, Win C is the expected number of categories won and Win M is the chance to win the match.

How the chances are computed

The same engine as the Rankings and Draft tabs. Every team in the league, yours and each opponent's, is built from its Active players (an opponent's bench counts as Active; IL players count on Rest of season only): a player counts for his remaining games (his team's schedule and the nightly lineup cap; on Rest of season also his projected availability, games over 82), turned into a season-equivalent total. The Per game / Rest of season toggle is the basis of these numbers, as on the Rankings tab. The engine then compares the team totals with its own noise, including its games-played variance. The Trade Analyzer uses the same forecast with both rosters changed (opponents weighted equally) but still counts Reserve players and treats IL players as healthy, so its Win M can differ from this tab's. In category leagues the chances are then pulled toward 50% (by 0.6 for the match, 0.75 for each category); points leagues are not pulled yet. In backtests of three seasons the raw rest-of-season odds were about twice too confident: matchups called 85% were won only 63-72% of the time. The backtest keeps draft rosters and preseason lines, so the true correction in a live league is probably a little smaller. Player gains scale by the same factors; the order of the lists does not change.

Win C and Win M per player

What he adds to your Win C (hundredths) and Win M (points) over an average player: your team minus a made-up average player plus him. The average player is the Plays %-weighted mean line of your Active players with your team's total games divided by your Active spots, so he is one slot's worth of your team; the incoming player's games are his own remaining games times the share of schedulable games your lineup gets. For a player already counted on your team it is what you lose if an average-line player with his games stood in his place; for Reserve (and IL on Per game) it is what he would add in Active. Same as Vs average in the player list.

Z and category weights

Z is the Rankings Zscore (Standard, per game). The Z button above the table swaps the stat columns for each category's weighted Z, which add up to the Z column. The weights are a punt lens: 1 counts a category fully, 0 leaves it out of the Z total. They only change Z and the Z order; Win C, Win M and the matrix always count every category.

Swapping players

Type part of a name in Send out (your roster) and Bring in (every other player), press Enter or click to add chips, then Apply swap. Any number can go out and in. Incoming players take Active while there is room, otherwise Reserve. A player taken from another team leaves that team, and the team receives the player you send out, in the slot he left (extra players go to its reserve; free agents send nothing back). Accents and capitals don't matter. Every change (a swap, a slot move, a drag) is listed under the cards as Changes with its effect on Win M; Undo last steps back one change and Clear list empties the list. The three cards under the boxes show your team now against the saved roster: Team Zscore, Win C and Win M, each with what it was and the change.

Player list

Every player, sorted by what he does to your Win M. The Change column is the real change in your Win M (points). Vs replacement: he takes the place of your own worst counted player (this is not the replacement level of the Rankings tab). Vs average: he takes the place of a made-up average player of your team, one Active slot's worth (for your own players: an average-line player with his games stands in his place). Win M is your chance after the change. All players are listed; use Show and Search to narrow it. The roster table above always uses Vs average.

A player already on your roster shows his gain over your worst player (the worst shows 0); his Win M after is that gain on top of your current Win M, an approximation. Click a row to bring him in (your own players go to Send out), or drag him onto a roster row to swap him into that slot, or onto a group header to add him there with no one going out. Category weights only re-sort the list, by the weighted change in category chances; the numbers shown stay real.
