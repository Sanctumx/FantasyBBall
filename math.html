Courtside Draft Kit: The Math Behind the Model

Updated 2026-10-17

The board turns projections (the average of Basketball Monster and Hashtag Basketball by default) and Fantrax ADP into one question at every pick: how much does this player raise your chance of winning matchups against the teams in your draft? Every team total is a mean plus a variance, and every probability is a normal CDF, written Φ.

1. Player value: Zscore

Zscore measures a player against an average drafted player, in units of how far apart team totals finish. Each player has a season value x in each category c:

  • Counting stats: per-game value × games (turnovers negated).

  • FG% and FT%: excess makes, (player % − league %) × attempts × games.

The board simulates 80 drafts, drawing each player's slot from his ADP and its spread (section 6), and records every team's season total T_c. Then:

zi,c=xi,c−xˉcSD(Tc)Zscorei=∑cWc zi,cz_{i,c} = \frac{x_{i,c} - \bar{x}_c}{\mathrm{SD}(T_c)} \qquad \mathrm{Zscore}_i = \sum_c W_c \, z_{i,c}zi,c​=SD(Tc​)xi,c​−xˉc​​Zscorei​=c∑​Wc​zi,c​

x̄_c is the average drafted player and W_c the category weight (×0 to ×2; TO starts at ×0.5, every other category at ×1). A small SD(T_c) means teams finish close together, so each unit of that stat earns more z. Weights change Zscore only; Win always counts every category. Zscore −1 and −2 leave out a player's worst one or two categories.

Games: Basis and IL spots

The games in x depend on Basis. The default, Season, uses BBM's projected games g as they are. BBM already expects injury-prone players to bounce back (its projected games follow last season's games with a slope of only 0.22), so counting them in full prices injury risk without over-penalizing it. Mix counts half of a player's projected missed games, measured from G₀, the average projected games among players drafted in a typical draft; Per game gives everyone G₀:

gSeason=ggMix=12 (g+G0)gPer game=G0g_{\text{Season}} = g \qquad g_{\text{Mix}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(g + G_0) \qquad g_{\text{Per game}} = G_0gSeason​=ggMix​=21​(g+G0​)gPer game​=G0​

With IL spots, a share f of the games a player misses (out of 80 a roster spot can fill) counts at waiver level instead of zero. Waiver level r is the average per-game line of the 20 players just past the end of the draft by ADP:

xc+=f⋅(80−g)⋅rcx_c \mathrel{+}= f \cdot (80 - g) \cdot r_cxc​+=f⋅(80−g)⋅rc​

Each extra spot adds less, because injuries overlap and short absences cannot use a spot. The curve follows real coverage on 2022-26 game logs (section 10), scaled so 2 spots sit at 0.70; backtests found no better share.

2. Team forecast

Every team's season total in each category is a mean plus a variance, built from the players it has drafted plus a typical player for each pick it still has:

μT=∑draftedxi+∑open picksmjVT=∑draftedσproj,i2+∑open picks(2sj2+σˉproj,j2)\mu_T = \sum_{\text{drafted}} x_i + \sum_{\text{open picks}} m_j \qquad V_T = \sum_{\text{drafted}} \sigma^2_{\text{proj},i} + \sum_{\text{open picks}} \left( 2 s_j^2 + \bar{\sigma}^2_{\text{proj},j} \right)μT​=drafted∑​xi​+open picks∑​mj​VT​=drafted∑​σproj,i2​+open picks∑​(2sj2​+σˉproj,j2​)

For the j-th pick still to come, m_j and s²_j are the weighted mean and variance of the available players near that spot. The weights are Gaussian in each player's expected slot, centered at j with width 1.5 + 0.12 × j; the factor 2 allows for teams that draft off ADP. V_T is large early and shrinks with every pick.

Each player also carries a projection variance for how far his season could miss:

This feeds team forecasts only; it does not lower a risky player's own value.

3. Weekly noise (H2H)

In H2H each category also gets a noise term for how much totals swing week to week; in Roto it is zero, since only season totals matter. A player's weekly variance in category k uses G = 3.42 × games / 82 expected games that week, his per-game mean m and his per-game variance v_k:

σweek2=G vk+0.82 m2\sigma^2_{\text{week}} = G \, v_k + 0.82 \, m^2σweek2​=Gvk​+0.82m2

3.42 is 82 games over 24 matchup weeks. The 0.82 term is the variance in how many games he plays in a week he plays, fitted on 628 drafted player-seasons (2022-26; his team's schedule alone gives 0.59, rest days and short absences the rest). The per-game variances are fitted on 723 player-seasons (46,381 games, 2023-24 to 2025-26); each multiplier held within about ±3% from season to season:

A team's weekly variance σ²_w,c is the sum over its players, averaged over the simulated teams. The gap between two teams in one week has variance 2σ²_w; in season units over 24 weeks:

noisec=2⋅242⋅σw,c2\text{noise}_c = 2 \cdot 24^2 \cdot \sigma^2_{w,c}noisec​=2⋅242⋅σw,c2​

Shared games factor

A team that plays more games in a week tends to win the counting stats together and lose TO. Each team's weekly totals share a games factor with variance n × 1.87 for n rostered players (measured on 2024-26 logs: the gap between two 12-player teams runs about ±6.7 games). Categories are independent given the games gap g between two teams, so the matchup chance averages over it at 5 Gauss-Hermite points:

M=∑kwk match ⁣(Φ ⁣(Δμc+Lc gkVA+VB+idioc))Lc=24⋅per-game‾cM = \sum_k w_k \, \text{match}\!\left( \Phi\!\left( \frac{\Delta\mu_c + L_c \, g_k}{\sqrt{V_A + V_B + \text{idio}_c}} \right) \right) \qquad L_c = 24 \cdot \overline{\text{per-game}}_cM=k∑​wk​match(Φ(VA​+VB​+idioc​​Δμc​+Lc​gk​​))Lc​=24⋅per-game​c​

On 2,730 team pairs from backtest rosters this gives a weekly Brier score of 0.2314 against real results.

4. Category win chance

The chance team A beats team B in category c is the expected gap divided by all the uncertainty:

P(A beats B in c)=Φ ⁣(μA−μBVA+VB+noisec)P(A \text{ beats } B \text{ in } c) = \Phi\!\left( \frac{\mu_A - \mu_B}{\sqrt{V_A + V_B + \text{noise}_c}} \right)P(A beats B in c)=Φ(VA​+VB​+noisec​​μA​−μB​​)

One more unit of the stat moves that chance by:

∂P∂μA=φ(z)VA+VB+noisec\frac{\partial P}{\partial \mu_A} = \frac{\varphi(z)}{\sqrt{V_A + V_B + \text{noise}_c}}∂μA​∂P​=VA​+VB​+noisec​​φ(z)​

A large noise term flattens the curve, so each unit added moves P less. That is why steals and points count for less in H2H, and assists, blocks and threes for more:

  • Points have by far the largest per-game variance, about 3 × the average, against 1.0–1.3 × for every other stat.

  • Steals are rare events, so a team's weekly total is mostly luck next to the gap between teams.

  • Assists, blocks and threes have a large gap between teams relative to their weekly noise.

Diminishing returns come from Φ itself: once P(win REB) is already 90%, the curve is flat there, so a third big man adds little.

5. Win C and Win M

Win compares your win chances with a candidate p at your next pick against a replacement-level player r in that slot, the best of what is left once the draft ends. Your forecast swaps r for p:

μ′=μbase−xr+xpV′=Vbase−σr2+σp2\mu' = \mu_{\text{base}} - x_r + x_p \qquad V' = V_{\text{base}} - \sigma^2_r + \sigma^2_pμ′=μbase​−xr​+xp​V′=Vbase​−σr2​+σp2​

Win C sums the category-by-category gains, averaged over your N − 1 opponents B, and the board shows it × 100 (72 is about three-quarters of a category win):

gc=mean⁡B[P(win c∣p)−P(win c∣r)]Win C=∑cgcg_c = \operatorname{mean}_B \big[ P(\text{win } c \mid p) - P(\text{win } c \mid r) \big] \qquad \text{Win C} = \sum_c g_cgc​=meanB​[P(win c∣p)−P(win c∣r)]Win C=c∑​gc​

In H2H most categories, each g_c is weighted by π_c / π̄, where π_c = P(win week | win c) − P(win week | lose c): how often category c decides the week.

Win M is the change in your chance of winning the whole matchup, in percentage points, averaged over opponents:

Win M=mean⁡B[M(p)−M(r)]M=∑kPr⁡(k wins)⋅{12k>n122k=n0otherwise\text{Win M} = \operatorname{mean}_B \big[ M(p) - M(r) \big] \qquad M = \sum_k \Pr(k \text{ wins}) \cdot \begin{cases} 1 & 2k > n \\ \tfrac{1}{2} & 2k = n \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}Win M=meanB​[M(p)−M(r)]M=k∑​Pr(k wins)⋅⎩⎨⎧​121​0​2k>n2k=notherwise​

Pr(k wins) comes from a Poisson-binomial over the per-category win chances P_c, with n categories, averaged over the shared games factor (section 3). Win M rewards close categories and gives little for ones you already win or cannot.

Playoff boost

A player's edge is his team's games in your playoff weeks divided by the league average, minus 1. B2B rest first removes the back-to-backs he tends to sit, from his rest rate on the last two seasons of logs (or your per-player override). Win and Zscore are multiplied by:

boost=clamp⁡(1+λ⋅edge,  0.5,  1.5)\text{boost} = \operatorname{clamp}\big(1 + \lambda \cdot \text{edge},\; 0.5,\; 1.5\big)boost=clamp(1+λ⋅edge,0.5,1.5)

6. Availability: There at, Lasts to, Pick range

A player's draft slot is normal around his Fantrax ADP. The spread is fitted on 50 real 12-team 9-cat Fantrax snake drafts (LOFB Bowl 2022, 2023 and 2024; 9,120 picks): about ±0.9 picks at the top, ±3.3 around pick 19, ±7.4 around pick 60 and ±11 around pick 106.

slot∼N(ADP,σ)σ=(0.42⋅ADP0.68)2+(0.05⋅ADP)2\text{slot} \sim \mathcal{N}(\text{ADP}, \sigma) \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\big(0.42 \cdot \text{ADP}^{0.68}\big)^2 + \big(0.05 \cdot \text{ADP}\big)^2}slot∼N(ADP,σ)σ=(0.42⋅ADP0.68)2+(0.05⋅ADP)2​

The first term is how far a player's picks scatter around his league's own consensus: the same in all three seasons and normal-shaped (69% of picks within one σ, about 2% beyond two σ on either side). The second term allows for a league's consensus sitting away from Fantrax's global ADP. Players without an ADP are treated as pick 175 with a wide spread, and Fantrax ADP past about 200 is not a real draft position.

The chance he is still on the board at pick k, and the chance he is still there at k given he is there now at pick c:

S(k)=1−Φ ⁣(k−0.5−ADPσ)P(there at k∣there now at c)=S(k)S(c)S(k) = 1 - \Phi\!\left( \frac{k - 0.5 - \text{ADP}}{\sigma} \right) \qquad P(\text{there at } k \mid \text{there now at } c) = \frac{S(k)}{S(c)}S(k)=1−Φ(σk−0.5−ADP​)P(there at k∣there now at c)=S(c)S(k)​

There at uses k = your next pick; Lasts to uses the pick after that. Pick range is ADP ± σ.

Edge

Edge is ADP minus the pick his value says he deserves, by the value chosen in Edge vs. Positive means he is going later than he should. An ADP past your draft's last pick counts as just after it.

Draft field

A sharper room takes good-value players earlier than ADP says. Draft field sets f, the share of opponents' picks made by value instead of ADP, from a mix of drafter types: f = (value drafters + 0.5 × sharp drafters) / 13, with ADP drafters and punters at 0. Easy (10 ADP, 2 sharp, 1 punter) gives f = 0.08; Average (5 ADP, 4 sharp, 2 punters, 2 value), the league every backtest uses, 0.31; Strong (2, 6, 2, 3) 0.46; Very strong (0, 6, 2, 5) 0.62. Each player's expected slot and its spread become:

slotE=(1−f) ADP+f (c+r)σE2=(1−f) σADP2+f σb2+f(1−f) (ADP−c−r)2\text{slot}_E = (1-f)\,\text{ADP} + f\,(c + r) \qquad \sigma_E^2 = (1-f)\,\sigma_{ADP}^2 + f\,\sigma_b^2 + f(1-f)\,(\text{ADP} - c - r)^2slotE​=(1−f)ADP+f(c+r)σE2​=(1−f)σADP2​+fσb2​+f(1−f)(ADP−c−r)2

c is the pick on the clock, r the player's rank by value among players left, and σ_b = 0.8 + 0.08 r. These feed There at, Lasts to, the Model pick and the typical players in every forecast.

Measured on real rooms, f sits between Easy and Average: 0.28 ± 0.07 for LOFB 2022 and 0.07 ± 0.04 for LOFB 2024 (section 10). Average stays the default; no level tested beat another.

7. Model pick and the # column

Off the clock, the badge reads Likely pick and uses Win M. For the top 10 candidates by Win M × There at: you get him if he is still there at your pick; if he is gone you take the best player left (F); and your following pick adds what is likely left then (E):

score=There at⋅Win M+(1−There at)⋅F+EE=∑omasso⋅qo⋅Win Mo\text{score} = \text{There at} \cdot \text{Win M} + (1 - \text{There at}) \cdot F + E \qquad E = \sum_o \text{mass}_o \cdot q_o \cdot \text{Win M}_oscore=There at⋅Win M+(1−There at)⋅F+EE=o∑​masso​⋅qo​⋅Win Mo​

E walks down the best other players o in order: q_o is the chance o lasts to your following pick, and mass_o the chance nobody better than o made it there. F is the same walk at your next pick. On the clock every available player is 100% there.

Two-pick look-ahead (on the clock)

When it is your pick, the board looks two picks ahead. For each of the 4 best players by Win M, the board re-runs with him on your roster and values your next pick as the expected Win M of the best player likely still there:

score(a)=Win M(a)+∑omasso⋅qo⋅Win Mo ∣ a rostered\text{score}(a) = \text{Win M}(a) + \sum_o \text{mass}_o \cdot q_o \cdot \text{Win M}_o \,\big|\, a \text{ rostered}score(a)=Win M(a)+o∑​masso​⋅qo​⋅Win Mo​​a rostered

The next pick is valued for the roster it will join, so overlaps and complements count. In the old test world (640 paired drafts) it won 2.7 ± 2.1 more titles per 100 seasons, made the playoffs 5.5 ± 1.5 points more often and won 2.2 ± 0.5 more matchups than Win M alone. It is the fit side of the Model pick on the clock, which leans on value only at your first picks (below).

Value early, then fit (on the clock)

On the clock the Model pick blends value and fit: score = (1 − w) × value + w × fit, with the fit share w = 1 / (1 + e^(−2 × (i − 1.5))) at your pick i (0 for your first). Value counts 95% at your first pick, 73% at your second, 27% at your third and 5% at your fourth; after that the pick is Win M + look-ahead. Value is half soft −1 Zscore and half Win C; fit is Win M + look-ahead (above), for the 4 best by Win M and the 4 best by value.

With real preseason projections and real ADP in the user's format (2022-26, 320 paired drafts, Punt Off), against plain Win M + look-ahead: titles +1.3 ± 1.5 per 100 seasons, prize +1.7 ± 0.9, playoffs made +3.1 ± 2.1, matchups +1.8 ± 0.5; in title equity (predicted title chance from matchup rate and playoffs made, fitted on 6,000 drafts) +1.4 ± 0.5. Other shapes against the same board: a sharper drop (k = 3) +0.9 ± 0.5 in combined title units; value at the first 2 picks only +0.8 ± 0.5; a slow fade (fit share t^0.25) +0.4 ± 0.5; the original √t slide lost (titles −2.2 ± 2.0, playoffs −9.1 ± 3.1). Win C alone as the value side, same k = 2 shape: titles −2.2 ± 1.5 and prize −1.6 ± 0.9 against the blend. Value helps at your first two picks and hurts when it still weighs in the middle rounds, where fit and availability decide. The √t slide that used to be the default is described in section 10.

The # column is a plain ranking by # order by (Win C, Win M or Zscore); it ignores availability. Grey names probably won't reach you.

8. Punt strategies

Plain Win forecasts your open picks as typical players at their ADP, so it charges a punt in full and never credits later picks that stack the other categories. Punt plans fix that. In 9-cat there are 130 plans S: no punt, 9 single punts, 36 double punts and 84 triple punts. The board no longer shows or uses them: Punt is always Off, with no Auto, no locking and no Strategy line. The plans stay in the engine for backtests.

For each plan, every open pick of yours is forecast from the players near that pick's expected slot, re-weighted toward the ones who fit it:

wi∝exp⁡ ⁣(−12(i−j1.5+0.12j)2)⋅exp⁡ ⁣(β∑c∉punt(S)zi,c)β=1.5w_i \propto \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{i - j}{1.5 + 0.12 j}\right)^2\right) \cdot \exp\!\left(\beta \sum_{c \notin \text{punt}(S)} z_{i,c}\right) \qquad \beta = 1.5wi​∝exp(−21​(1.5+0.12ji−j​)2)⋅exp​βc∈/punt(S)∑​zi,c​​β=1.5

V(S) is your average matchup win chance with that forecast. Win_S is computed as in section 5 on top of it, with punted categories counting zero in Win C. Punt Auto (backtests only now, see below) blends 34 plans: no punt, the 9 single punts and the 24 most synergistic double punts:

wS=exp⁡(V(S)/τ)∑S′exp⁡(V(S′)/τ)Win=∑SwS WinSτ=0.01w_S = \frac{\exp(V(S)/\tau)}{\sum_{S'} \exp(V(S')/\tau)} \qquad \text{Win} = \sum_S w_S \, \text{Win}_S \qquad \tau = 0.01wS​=∑S′​exp(V(S′)/τ)exp(V(S)/τ)​Win=S∑​wS​WinS​τ=0.01

The 8 plans with the most weight get a full Win pass. Early in a draft the V(S) values sit within about a point of each other, so the blend stays near neutral; as your roster commits, one plan pulls ahead and takes over. In practice Auto mostly chooses between no punt and a single punt. Locking a plan sets its weight to 1.

The board never punts. With real preseason projections and real ADP in the user's league format (2022-26, 320 paired drafts), not punting beat Auto: titles +2.5 ± 1.9 per 100 seasons, playoffs made +9.4 ± 2.9, matchups +3.6 ± 0.6. With Win M + look-ahead, Punt Off on top of no slide adds +1.3 ± 1.9 titles (current board: titles 10.6%, playoffs 65.9%, matchups 50.9%, against 7.2%, 56.6% and 48.0% for the slide with Auto). Punting paid off only in the old test world, where a noisy market left whole player types underpriced. Auto and every plan remain available to lock by hand.

Re-tested on the current Model pick (value early with k = 2, then Win M + look-ahead), 160 paired drafts against Punt Off: Auto as it is lost titles −9.4 ± 2.5 per 100 seasons, playoffs made −6.9 ± 3.9, matchups −3.3 ± 0.9 (combined −4.5 ± 0.8); Auto with a commitment that firms up from τ 0.05 to 0.01 by your sixth pick did the same (combined −4.3 ± 0.9). An opportunistic punt (from your sixth pick, lock your weakest category once its win chance is under 25%) rarely triggers and changes nothing that holds up: title equity +0.3 ± 0.2 on the first 160 drafts, −0.04 ± 0.08 on the other 160, +0.14 ± 0.11 over all 320, and it changed the draft in only 17 of 320. So the Punt setting was removed from the board. Planned punting costs more with value-first early picks, because the value picks already build a balanced core that a punt then wastes.

9. How the board is tested

Every setting is judged in simulated drafts of the user's league, paired draft by draft against the current board, and scored on what really happened that season. Results are titles per 100 seasons, expected prize, playoffs made and matchup-win-rate points, ± one standard error.

  • Seasons: 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26, with real preseason projections from the user's draft kits (Hashtag in 2022-23 and 2023-24, the BBM/Hashtag average in 2024-25, BBM in 2025-26) and real preseason ADP as the market (Hashtag's rank in 2023-24, which has no ADP). The player pool is everyone with 20+ games the season before, so rookies are missing.

  • League: the user's format: 14 teams, third-round reversal, H2H each category, 3 IL spots, playoff boost Light, Draft field Average.

  • Opponents: 13 per league, 2 drafting with this kit, 2 punters, 4 sharp drafters ranking players by a 50/50 blend of value rank and ADP, and 5 drafting from ADP with the fitted spread from section 6. Everyone sees the same projections; when a test changes only the board's projections, the rest of the league keeps its own.

  • Board team: takes the Model pick at every pick, from slots 1, 4, 7, 10 and 14; 80 drafts per season for the main comparisons (320 in all), 40 for screening.

  • Scoring: real box scores. Daily lineups of 10 active out of 12, 3 IL spots (a player out the previous and current week goes to IL and the best free agent fills his spot), and 4 adds a week streamed on each team's two weakest spots, aimed at the categories it is losing or close in.

  • Titles: weeks 1-17 rank the teams by share of categories won, the top 8 make the playoffs, and 2-2-2 rounds (weeks 18-23) decide the title on summed totals, most categories winning. The current board wins 11.9% of titles, makes the playoffs 69% of the time and wins 52.6% of matchups (an average team: 7.1%, 57% and 50%). Expected prize uses 60 / 30 / 5 + 5 for champion, runner-up and semifinal losers.

  • Earlier tests projected each season from the previous season's stats and drew ADP from that same noisy value. That world was far easier (the board made the playoffs 94% of the time) and favored grabbing value and punting, which is why the value-to-fit slide and Punt Auto were once the defaults. Results from it that real projections overturned are marked where they appear; the rest of section 10 comes from it unless it says otherwise.

Title equity and the combined effect

Titles are a noisy yes/no: over 320 drafts their standard error is about ±2 per 100 seasons, larger than most effects being tested. Title equity is a steadier stand-in in the same units. A logistic fit on every real-projection backtest draft (6,000) gives a team's title chance from its matchup win rate m and whether it made the playoffs:

logit P(title) = −29.4 + 12.2 × m + 20.2 × made

For a playoff team that is 2.4% at m = 0.45, 4.3% at 0.50, 7.6% at 0.55 and 13.1% at 0.60. Each test is scored by the change in predicted title chance, paired draft by draft; its standard error is about a third of the actual-titles one (±0.5 against ±1.5 to 2). The combined effect blends actual titles and title equity, each weighted by one over its variance, so it stays in titles per 100 seasons and leans on whichever is more precise. Equity only sees regular-season strength; playoff-week strength (each team against every other over the playoff weeks) is now recorded and will be added to the fit, so changes that trade regular-season wins for playoff strength are credited.

The other settings were re-checked the same way, against the board with Punt Off (real projections, the user's format; combined effect in titles per 100 seasons, 320 paired drafts unless noted): playoff boost Off instead of Light +0.9 ± 0.5 (the board now defaults to Off); Medium −1.0 ± 0.7 (160); Draft field None instead of Average +0.7 ± 0.6; Strong +0.1 ± 0.8 (160); IL fill 60% instead of the curve's 79% at 3 spots 0.0 ± 0.7, 50% −0.2, 70% −0.6; Mix basis instead of Season −1.0 ± 1.1 (160); a look-ahead over 8 candidates instead of 4 −0.2 ± 0.2 (160). None clears two standard errors, so the settings stay.

Young players' upside. Do 2nd- and 3rd-year players drafted late beat their projections by enough to be worth reaching for? For 2022-26, each player's actual season value (9-cat season-total z, scaled on the top 168 projected players) was set against the preseason projection the backtests use. A late pick who busts gets dropped, so his value to you is the higher of his actual value and waiver level (the average of actual ranks 169-190, about −5.1 in every season). Upside is the average amount he beat his projection by; downside is the average shortfall after that drop floor. In rounds 7-12 (14 teams), 2nd- and 3rd-year players had upside 0.54 and downside 1.24, net −0.63 ± 0.27 against their projection; veterans had upside 0.64 and downside 1.27, net −0.47 ± 0.13. Young minus veterans: −0.17 ± 0.30 in rounds 7-12, −0.31 ± 0.36 in rounds 10-12 and −1.04 ± 0.64 in rounds 1-6. Young players have less upside, not more: the projections already build in their growth. The drop floor credits a full season of waiver value, more generous than real in-season drops, and there is still no edge, so the board gives no youth bonus. Rookies are untested, because the backtest pool only holds players with a previous NBA season. NBA experience comes from player ids, which can be a year off around the 2022 and 2023 drafts.

The current settings are the ones nothing beat on fresh drafts; section 10 lists what was tried.

A negative number would mean teams reach for what they need. Teams with no center yet don't take centers earlier, so availability keeps one ADP curve for every opponent.

Real drafters rarely punt. On the same drafts (600 teams against 12,000 round-shuffled rosters), 18.8% of real teams finish a category 2 sd or more below their division, against 11.9% by chance, so about 7% punt one category on purpose: mostly FT%, then 3PM, TO, AST and FG%. Double punts don't show up beyond chance (15.0% vs 13.5% with two categories under −1.5 sd). That is about one punter in a 14-team league.

Giannis is the clear exception. On the 50 real teams that drafted him, his teammates' FT% sits at −1.31 sd against −0.46 for shuffled rosters, and 68% of those teams are below −1 sd against 28% by chance: his drafters punt FT on purpose. The board does not yet expect that from Giannis teams, and the test punters (section 9) reach only −1.09 sd in their punted categories, softer than real punters at −2 sd or more.

Draft field levels are now measured, roughly. Within a room, teams don't split into ADP drafters and value drafters: each team's tilt toward last season's value spreads no wider than when each round's picks are shuffled among the teams (sd 0.163 vs 0.174 in 2022, 0.121 vs 0.121 in 2023, 0.147 vs 0.136 in 2024), so one value weight f describes the room. Scoring each room's consensus slot against the preseason ADP and projections in the user's kits, slot = (1 − f)·ADP + f·value slot, gives f = 0.28 ± 0.07 for LOFB 2022 (30 divisions, Hashtag projections) and 0.07 ± 0.04 for LOFB 2024 (10 divisions, BBM/Hashtag average), the same on players whose value and ADP differ by 15+ slots. That is Easy to Average, never Strong. The kits' ADP is Hashtag's, not Fantrax's, and players are matched by name through today's Fantrax list (103 and 131 of the top 150); a 2026 draft scored against that day's Fantrax ADP snapshot will pin it down.

Value-to-fit slide (old test world)

Before the real-projection tests, the Model pick used a slower slide from value to fit. Early picks should take the most value; late picks should fill what the roster needs. The slide blends the two with a weight that grows through the draft:

score = (1 − w) × value + w × fit, w = √t, t = your pick number / your last pick (0 at your first pick, 1 at your last)

Value and fit are each standardized over the 40 best players left. Value is a 50/50 blend of soft −1 Zscore and Win C. Soft −1 is Zscore with the player's worst category counting half when it is negative, so one weak category costs less than it does in full Zscore but more than under a punt. Fit is Win M plus the two-pick look-ahead (section 7), for the 4 best players by Win M and the 4 best by value.

Prize equity (60 / 30 / 5 for first, second and a semifinal, fitted like title equity) agrees in sign with title equity on every test run so far, so no decision changes with the prize structure.
